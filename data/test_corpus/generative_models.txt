Generative Models and Modern Generative AI

Introduction
Generative models learn the underlying distribution of data so they can create new samples such as text, images, audio, or code. Modern generative AI systems are built on foundations from probabilistic modeling and deep learning.

Classical and Modern Families:
1. Autoregressive Models: Predict the next token or pixel step by step
2. Variational Autoencoders (VAEs): Learn a latent representation for generation
3. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs): Use a generator and discriminator in competition
4. Diffusion Models: Learn to reverse a noise process to produce realistic outputs
5. Large Language Models (LLMs): Transformer-based models for text generation

Important Concepts:
- Latent space
- Sampling strategy
- Prompt conditioning
- Temperature and diversity control
- Alignment and safety filtering

Applications:
- Text generation and summarization
- Image synthesis and editing
- Speech generation
- Code completion
- Educational assistants and tutoring systems

Risks and Limitations:
- Hallucination and factual error
- Bias in generated content
- Copyright and data provenance concerns
- High computational cost
- Difficulty in evaluating generation quality

Generative AI is highly relevant to AI-Pedia because it can support explanation, summarization, and interactive learning, but it should be used carefully alongside trustworthy external resources.
