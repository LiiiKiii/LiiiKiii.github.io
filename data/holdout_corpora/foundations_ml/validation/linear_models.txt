Linear Models in Machine Learning

Linear models are among the simplest and most interpretable tools in machine learning. They assume that predictions can be expressed as a weighted combination of input features.

Two foundational examples are:
- linear regression for continuous outputs
- logistic regression for binary classification

Important properties:
1. Easy to train and explain
2. Strong baselines for many problems
3. Sensitive to feature scaling and representation quality
4. Often regularized using L1 or L2 penalties

Why linear models remain important:
- they provide interpretable coefficients
- they are computationally efficient
- they establish a baseline before trying more complex models
- they help explain the idea of decision boundaries and optimization

Limitations:
Linear models cannot naturally capture complex nonlinear relationships unless feature engineering or kernel methods are introduced. Even so, they are central to introductory AI and machine learning because they connect statistical reasoning, optimization, and practical prediction in a simple framework.
