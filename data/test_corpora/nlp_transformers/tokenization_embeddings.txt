Tokenization and Embeddings in NLP

Natural language processing systems cannot work directly on raw text strings. They first convert language into discrete tokens and then into vector representations called embeddings.

Tokenization options include:
- word-level tokenization
- subword tokenization such as BPE or WordPiece
- character-level tokenization

Embeddings map tokens into continuous vector spaces where semantically related items are close together. Important ideas include:
1. distributed representations
2. context-independent embeddings such as word2vec or GloVe
3. contextual embeddings from transformers
4. positional information for token order

Why this matters:
Good tokenization and embedding design affects vocabulary coverage, efficiency, and downstream model quality. Subword methods are especially useful because they handle rare words, morphology, and open-vocabulary text more effectively than fixed whole-word vocabularies.

Modern large language models build on these ideas by combining tokenization, embedding layers, and deep contextual processing inside a transformer architecture.
