Language Model Pretraining

Pretraining is the stage where a language model learns broad statistical and semantic patterns from large text corpora before being adapted to a downstream task.

Common objectives:
- next-token prediction in autoregressive models
- masked token prediction in bidirectional models
- contrastive or span-based variants in some architectures

Why pretraining matters:
1. it transfers knowledge across tasks
2. it reduces the amount of labelled data needed later
3. it supports strong general-purpose representations
4. it enables large language models to perform zero-shot or few-shot tasks

After pretraining, models are often fine-tuned or instruction-tuned for specific applications such as question answering, summarization, or conversational assistance.

In educational systems, pretrained models are attractive because they provide broad knowledge and fluent explanations. However, they still require grounding, evaluation, and sometimes retrieval augmentation to ensure trustworthiness.
